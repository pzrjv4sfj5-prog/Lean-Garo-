# Thangseng — Full Question Batch (2026-07-25)
For Tridip to relay in one session. Grouped by topic. Each question
notes which project item it closes.

## A. "Go" — remaining gap (NV-001, partially answered before)
1. "I am not going" (no destination) — what's the correct Garo?
2. "I am not going to the market" (with destination) — and this one?
3. "He did not go" (no destination) vs. "He did not go to the market"
   (with destination) — is the verb form different between these two?
   (We already know `re·` and `re·ang` are both used for "go" — we
   need to know if the destination is what decides which one.)

## B. Predication / "is X" — remaining gap (NV-002, partially answered before)
4. Take the word "happy": say "I am happy," "you are happy," and "he
   is happy" — in each one, is there a word for "is" (like `ong·a`),
   or is it just the adjective by itself? Does it change between "I,"
   "you," and "he"?

## C. Locative words — 9 words needing real sentences (NV-003)
5. Please give one full sentence for each: "the book is below the
   table," "...inside the box," "...outside the house," "...above the
   shelf," "...behind the door," "...beside the window," "...up on the
   roof," "...over the bridge," "...across the river."
   (We already have a confirmed sentence for "under the table" — these
   are the other 9 we're missing.)

## D. "Under" vs "beneath a covering" (NV-004)
6. "The book is under the blanket" — same word as "the dog is under
   the table," or a different word?
7. "The rock is under the water" — same word again, or different?

## E. Necessity ("need to") vs. desire ("want to") (NV-005)
8. "I don't want to watch TV" — how do you say that?
9. "I don't need to watch TV" — how do you say that? Is the word
   different from #8, or the same?

## F. Infinitive forms — raka check (NV-010)
10. When you say "want to speak" or "want to study," is there a `·`
    mark in the verb — even though there isn't one when you just say
    "I spoke" or "I was studying"?
11. Is there a different word that sounds like `agan·`/`tus·` (with
    the `·` mark), separate from `agan`/`tusi` (without it)?

## G. "Watch"/"see"/"look" — one root or several? (NV-011, NV-012)
12. Is the word for "I see it," "I want to watch it," and "I am
    watching" all the same root word with different endings? Or is
    "watch" (like watching TV) a completely different word from
    general "see/look"?
13. For "I am watching TV" — does it sound right to swap in `nisona`
    instead of `nina`? Does it change the meaning?

## H. Posture verb "lying down" (NV-007)
14. "I am lying down" (no bed mentioned)
15. "I am lying in bed"
16. "I was lying down" (past)
17. "I am not lying down" (negative)

## I. Ability "can" (NV-008)
18. "I can eat" — how do you say that?
19. "I can go" — and that?
20. "I can work" — and that?
21. Separately: does `ama` ever mean "can" (ability), or does it only
    mean "mother"? If it can mean both, is it really the same word
    said the same way, or could "can" actually be a different word
    that just sounds similar?

## J. Loanwords for modern things (NV-009)
22. How do you say "TV" in a Garo sentence — the English word as-is,
    or is there a Garo way of writing/saying it?
23. Same question for "status" (like a phone/social-media status).
24. Are there other everyday English words like this (phone, internet)
    that Garo speakers just use as-is? Any examples that come to mind?

## K. "Need" vs "want" — related words? (NV-016)
25. Is `nanga` ("need") related to `nangja` ("need not") — same word,
    just made negative? Or are they unrelated?
26. Is `ska` ("want") just a shorter/casual way of saying `sikenga`, or
    a genuinely different word?

## L. "Angry" — six candidate words (NV-027)
We already confirmed `ka·o·nang·a` is the everyday word for angry.
These six other words came up in older data — for each, is it a real
word for a different shade/intensity of angry, or not a real word at
all (a mistake/mis-hearing)?
27. `Ka-a chakna amja`
28. `Ka-a soa`
29. `bi·ka so·a`
30. `hel·hel`
31. `Ka-chaa`

## M. "Tied/bound" pattern — does it generalize? (NV-029)
We know "the dog is tied" = `Achakko kae donenga` (literally
"dog-having-tied placed"). Does the same pattern work for other
words?
32. "The picture is hung [on the wall]" — how do you say that?
33. "The key is hidden" — how do you say that?
34. "The cup is broken" — how do you say that?

## N. `ong·ja` vs `dongja` — free choice or not? (NV-030)
35. You gave us both `ong·ja` and `dongja` for "is not" (as in "the
    book is not on the table"). Can you use either one any time, or
    does it depend on the sentence — like formality, or who you're
    talking to?

## O. Question marker `-ma` beyond future tense (NV-031)
36. "Did you eat?" (past) — how do you ask that?
37. "Are you eating?" (present) — and that?
38. "Do you want the book?" (present, with an object) — and that?

## P. "Hot" — two more candidate words (NV-034)
39. Is `gek·gek` a real word for "hot"? If so, when would you use it
    instead of `ding·a`?
40. Is `jro·a` a real word for "hot"? Same question — when would you
    use it?

## Q. "Breakfast" loanword (NV-038)
41. Is `na·sta` (sometimes written `nastha`) a word you'd actually use
    for "breakfast," even if it's borrowed from another language?
42. Is there any other Garo way to say "breakfast" — even a
    descriptive phrase, like "morning food"?
